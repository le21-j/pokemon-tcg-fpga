Inspected November 9, 2026, at commit `8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428` in the read-only clone `/tmp/openjev-review.Bm8EwN`. This is source inspection, not a successful installation or benchmark. No project code, model download, or training was executed.

Project direction update, November 12: the [current plan](openjev-tcg-adaptation-plan.md) trains an independent Deep Sets position evaluator on Sol and deploys incremental evaluation on the DE10-Lite with PC search. The SDK findings below record the earlier teacher/distillation alternative.

## Verified contracts and dataset behavior

`openjev.tod.schema.Decision` contains `id`, `kind`, `state`, `question`, candidate tuple, optional `target`, `group_id`, and spec fingerprint. `state` must be JSON-compatible string, dictionary, or list. `Candidate.text` is model-visible; `payload` is the object the caller executes and is not rendered. Validation inherits the v1 requirement of 2 through 255 candidates with unique nonempty IDs and texts. A forced single choice therefore needs a host bypass or a deliberate schema change. [schema.py, lines 18–23 and 100–165](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/schema.py#L18-L165)

`DecisionSpec` owns enumeration. `decision(..., choice=...)` uses a candidate ID, while `taken=...` matches a payload and records `NoneCorrect` when no candidate matches. `check()` regenerates candidates and compares them with the stored tuple. Its fingerprint covers declared names, questions, versions, and defer IDs, not the body of an arbitrary Python callback. Candidate-generation changes therefore require a version bump. `ListSpec` can persist as `spec.json` and excludes its candidate-list field from the encoder's shared context. A custom code-defined spec must be supplied again when loading. [spec.py, lines 25–106 and 126–168](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/spec.py#L25-L168)

`build_dataset()` validates rows, filters episodes by optional `min_outcome`, drops identical decision inputs, counts conflicting labels, and hashes groups into splits. Defaults are 70% training and 10% each validation, calibration, and test. A row's explicit `group_id` overrides the episode ID, so a Pokémon adapter must assign the complete BO3 match consistently. `Dataset.write()` writes v2 JSONL, Choice-only v1 bridge files, a manifest, and a serializable spec when available. Outcome filtering is a data-selection policy, not reinforcement learning. [spec.py, lines 175–253](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/spec.py#L175-L253)

The target union is `Choice`, `Subset`, `Preference`, `Outcome`, and `NoneCorrect`. There is no teacher-probability-distribution target. Frozen-head and TOD-picker supervised training consume Choice rows; encoder training consumes Choice and Subset rows, and rejects mixing those target types within one kind. Preference and Outcome being parseable does not make them implemented supervised losses. [schema.py, lines 32–97](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/schema.py#L32-L97), [encoder.py, lines 91–101 and 323–340](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/encoder.py#L91-L340)

## Training methods, input limits, and selection semantics

The CLI exposes three distinct methods: `head`, the default, learns small heads over a frozen Gemma backbone; `tod` fine-tunes a Gemma-based picker through LoRA; `encoder` fine-tunes a Hugging Face trunk and per-candidate head. Only encoder accepts its batching, layout, loss, checkpoint, and length flags. `--targets` sets Subset survival-calibration targets, not soft training labels. [cli.py, lines 46–120 and 143–145](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/cli.py#L46-L145)

The TOD picker has a stricter 26-candidate letter limit and an 8,192-token default prompt budget. It raises an error for oversized prompts instead of truncating. Treating it as an unrestricted legal-action scorer would be incorrect. [picker.py, lines 93–105 and 137–160](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/picker.py#L93-L160)

Encoder `query_text()` renders the question and spec-selected state. In `pairs` layout, each candidate forms a separate tokenized pair with longest-first truncation. In `packed`, candidates have independent masked spans with restarted positions. Candidate text defaults to a 4,000-character cap; packed queries retain at most half `max_length`, and each candidate span is shortened to fit its query-plus-candidate allowance. The entire concatenated packed decision can exceed that allowance because it contains many spans. Dense attention-mask allocation consequently needs a realistic worst-case memory pilot. [encoder.py, lines 72–88](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/encoder.py#L72-L88), [packing.py, lines 43–80 and 112–128](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/packing.py#L43-L128)

Packed scoring aims to make an individual candidate's logit independent of sibling candidates and their presentation order. This does not provide an ordered action decoder. `Subset` instead learns independent sigmoid membership and serves a thresholded kept set. It supplies no Pokémon-specific `minCount`, `maxCount`, repeated-choice, or ordered-effect semantics. A host selection adapter must implement those, or encode complete valid action lists as candidate payloads with explicit coverage limits. [packing.py, lines 5–16](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/packing.py#L5-L16), [schema.py, lines 51–61 and 179–198](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/schema.py#L51-L198)

## Actual RL and simulator integration

`Environment` requires `reset(seed)`, `observe()->(kind,state)|None`, `act(payload)`, `defer()`, and terminal `reward()`. `HeadPolicy.rollout()` samples when given an RNG and otherwise uses argmax. Missing heads defer. These are generic interfaces; no CABT environment adapter is supplied by the inspected cookbook. [rl.py, lines 35–40 and 117–172](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/rl.py#L35-L172)

The implemented algorithms are `reinforce()`, using normalized episode-return advantages, and `improve()`, using deterministic-seed replay to try alternative candidates and learn improved hard labels. `improve()` includes supervised anchoring and a validation-accuracy rollback guard. It explicitly requires deterministic resets and notes that earlier branches can exploit information unavailable at the decision. Neither algorithm is PPO. Their rollout collection is a serial Python loop, not a distributed actor system. [rl.py, lines 267–384](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/rl.py#L267-L384)

RL updates frozen-backbone heads or the TOD picker's shared LoRA adapter. Encoder policies implement `trainer()` by raising `NotImplementedError` for `improve/reinforce`. The proposed encoder-teacher-plus-RL path therefore needs new implementation or an outer loop that generates new labeled rows and reruns supervised encoder training. [picker.py, lines 350–401](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/picker.py#L350-L401), [encoder.py, lines 1026–1028](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/encoder.py#L1026-L1028)

The ShopDesk cookbook demonstrates the adapter boundary clearly: `trace_to_episode()` reconstructs model-visible history and labels executed payloads; `build_dataset(..., min_outcome=1.0)` retains successful conversations; step 5 calls `improve(policy, ShopDesk, ...)`. Pokémon needs its own replay converter, observation renderer, candidate mapping, opponent controller, terminal return, and fallback behavior. [adapter.py, lines 19–44](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/cookbooks/02-shopdesk-traces-to-rl/adapter.py#L19-L44), [step2_dataset.py](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/cookbooks/02-shopdesk-traces-to-rl/step2_dataset.py#L1-L11), [step5_rl.py](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/cookbooks/02-shopdesk-traces-to-rl/step5_rl.py#L1-L13)

## Sol execution, recovery, and independent student work

PyTorch/CUDA is available through backend selection. Encoder fine-tuning detects `torchrun` ranks, uses DDP, and distributes token-budgeted decisions. Periodic checkpoints are loadable model directories, but continued training creates fresh AdamW, scheduler, epoch plan, and RNG progression. This is weight continuation, not exact interrupted-run recovery. No equivalent distributed RL orchestration is established by this source. [compute.py, lines 18–45](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/compute.py#L18-L45), [encoder.py, lines 344–403 and 483–597](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/openjev/tod/encoder.py#L344-L597)

Source tests cover spec drift, grouped data construction, payload isolation, Subset round trips, model continuation/export, candidate-order independence, and token/rank planning. They were read, not run. They do not establish CABT compatibility or Sol throughput. [test_tod.py, lines 46–133](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/tests/test_tod.py#L46-L133), [test_tod_encoder.py, lines 190–237](https://github.com/daseinlabs/open-jev/blob/8f2ced8f93bd43d09f081bbe5c3c2e13d37b3428/tests/test_tod_encoder.py#L190-L237)

Proposed new work: export teacher probabilities with IDs, player perspective, engine/spec version, and candidate order into a separate distillation dataset. Train an independent tiny numeric-feature scorer with its own loss and integer exporter. OpenJEV has no soft-label target or tiny FPGA-student training path here. Its encoder export retains the transformer trunk and head; extracting that head alone does not remove its feature dependency. Keep this student pipeline separate, then compare complete-game quality and end-to-end hardware latency against the teacher.
